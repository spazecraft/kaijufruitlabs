    言WHAT KOTOBA MEANS

    Kotoba (言葉, ことば) means "word" or "words." It can also mean language, or the way someone speaks, like "kind words" or the words of a song.

    言to say, speech
    +
    葉leaf
    =
    言葉speech leaves

    So the word for "word" is written as speech leaves.

    歌THE OLDEST PICTURE

    More than 1,100 years ago, the poet Ki no Tsurayuki wrote the preface to the Kokinshū, a famous collection of Japanese poems. He described poetry as a plant growing out of the heart:

    やまとうたは、人の心を種として、よろづの言の葉とぞなれりける。
    Yamato uta wa, hito no kokoro o tane to shite, yorozu no koto no ha to zo narerikeru.
    "Japanese poetry takes the human heart as its seed, and grows into countless leaves of words."
    Ki no Tsurayuki, preface to the Kokinshū, around 905

    That old form, 言の葉 (koto no ha), "leaves of words," is still used in poetry today.
